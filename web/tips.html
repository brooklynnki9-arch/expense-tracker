Budget tips

1. Write down everything for one month

Before you set any budgets, just record every purchase for a month, even small ones like snacks or bus fare. Small things add up, and the report will show you where your money really goes.

2. Try the 50/30/20 rule

A popular starting point is to split the money you earn like this:

  • 50% for needs, like rent, food, and getting to work or school
  • 30% for wants, like eating out, games, and clothes
  • 20% for savings or paying off debt

For example, if you earn $2,000 a month, that's $1,000 for needs, $600 for wants, and $400 for savings. It's a guide, not a rule, so change it to fit your life.

3. Set budgets you can actually keep

Start with budgets close to what you already spend, then lower them a little each month. A budget that's too strict is easy to give up on. The tracker warns you when you go over, so you can fix it before the month ends.

4. Look at your biggest category first

The report marks the category where you spend the most. Cutting 10% there usually saves more than cutting everything else combined.

5. Pay yourself first

When you get paid, move your savings out right away, before you start spending. What's left is what you can spend this month.

6. Check in once a week

Pick one day a week to open the tracker and look at the month so far. Five minutes a week is enough to stay on track.

These are general tips to help you get started, not professional financial advice.
